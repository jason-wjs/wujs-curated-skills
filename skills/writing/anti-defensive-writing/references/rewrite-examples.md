# Anti-defensive rewrite examples

Adapted from Kiterlin's examples in academic-writing-skills; see the skill's
license files. Preserve evidential force and useful scope even when the clearest
revision still states a limitation. Supplied facts are the limit of the edit.

## Sentence polishing

Before: “This may suggest that X could potentially influence Y.”

After: “This suggests that X may influence Y.”

This removes stacked hedges without turning a suggestion into proof.

Before: “We do not claim that these cases are representative of all contexts.”

After: “These cases are not necessarily representative of other contexts.”

The representativeness limitation survives. Do not invent positive scope such
as three institutional settings. Retain the original when it already fits.

Before: “This paper is not intended to provide a comprehensive theory of
platform governance, but rather to examine one specific mechanism.”

Minimal edit: “This paper examines one mechanism of platform governance rather
than providing a comprehensive theory.”

For paragraph restructuring, “This paper examines one mechanism of platform
governance” is enough if the comprehensive-theory contrast is redundant in context.

## Academic introduction

Before: “Although this preliminary inquiry cannot resolve all debates about
platform governance, it might cautiously be suggested that algorithmic content
moderation could potentially suppress participation. Our observational sample
may suffer from unobserved confounding. We do not claim universal applicability.”

After: “This preliminary inquiry examines whether algorithmic content moderation
suppresses participation. The observational sample may be affected by unobserved
confounding, and the findings do not establish universal applicability.”

The edit states the question early and preserves both evidence constraints.
No effect size, platform name, or causal design is added.

## Methods and contributions

Before: “We do not claim that our architecture is superior on all benchmarks or
solves quadratic memory complexity. Given training costs, evaluation is restricted
to smaller baselines. We attempt to introduce a sparse block-diagonal approximation
that might offer latency improvements at intermediate sequence lengths.”

After: “We introduce a sparse block-diagonal approximation that may improve latency
at intermediate sequence lengths. Evaluation uses smaller baselines because of
training costs. The method does not establish universal benchmark superiority or
a solution to quadratic memory complexity.”

Delete the final sentence only if it is redundant in the surrounding text; do
not replace the unestablished benefit with measured improvements.

## Grant aim

Before: “Despite weather disturbances and possible calibration drift, we hope
surface-temperature logging might help guide targeted tree planting.”

After: “The project aims to use surface-temperature logging to guide targeted
tree planting, accounting for weather disturbances and possible calibration drift.”

The aim is clearer, but remains a plan. Do not add node counts, accuracy targets,
or completed validation. A reviewer response may need an explicit non-claim;
use the rebuttal skill for that genre.
