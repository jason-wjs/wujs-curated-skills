# Editing scope and voice

The user's explicit instructions override these defaults. Interpret scope from
what they ask to edit, not from how much context they supply.

- Sentence polishing: preserve the sentence's structure by default; fix grammar,
  usage, awkward wording, and redundancy. If the structure prevents a clear
  repair, give the minimal edit first and an optional restructured alternative.
- Paragraph polishing: allow splitting or combining sentences, changing their
  order, and reorganizing the requested passage. For several paragraphs, allow
  redistribution within that range. Do not extend the edit to surrounding text.
- Make an already implied logical connection explicit only when the supplied
  material supports it. Put gaps requiring new premises, assumptions, evidence,
  or author decisions outside the revised prose.
- Preserve facts, argument, evidential strength, essential scope, technical
  terms, notation, citations, and the status of planned versus completed work.
  Do not silently resolve ambiguous scientific meaning or add results.

Write the point directly, with enough context to follow the reasoning. Remove
empty importance claims, reflexive defenses against imagined critics, canned
contrasts, and repeated summaries. Preserve substantive contrasts, negative
results, and uncertainty. Necessary qualifications may recur where passages
must stand alone, including abstracts and conclusions.

Let sentence length and paragraph structure follow the argument. Keep stable
technical terms rather than cycling synonyms. Respect the author's supplied
samples and the genre; no blanket word, punctuation, sentence-count, or list
quota applies. Strong existing prose need not change.

Return the requested revision, plus consequential changes or unresolved gaps
when useful. For diagnosis-only requests, give findings without rewriting.
Do not run a sequence of style skills by default. Consult a focused reference
only for a problem present in the requested passage, then check the revision
against its source for changes in meaning.
